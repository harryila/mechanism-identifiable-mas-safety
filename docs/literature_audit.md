# Literature audit: local-allow/global-harm mechanisms

Audit date: 2026-08-31

This audit checks the eight arXiv references named in the project brief and adds four closely related preprints. Every bibliographic claim below was checked against the paper's canonical arXiv record and full text. The mechanism and evaluation fields are audit codings, not quotations from the authors. All twelve sources are preprints; this audit does not assert peer-review status or independently reproduce their results.

## Bottom line

- All eight arXiv identifiers in the brief resolve to real papers.
- Seven brief labels are recognizable title fragments or shorthands. One is bibliographically mismatched: arXiv:2505.21184 is titled *Jailbreak-as-a-Service++*, while **PoisonSwarm** is the framework introduced inside that paper.
- The papers do not all identify the same causal phenomenon. Only *MasDrift* includes a clear same-task single-agent versus multi-agent architecture comparison. Several others study multi-turn interaction, repeated stateless calls, cross-provider model combinations, or single-agent tool mediation.
- “Policy” also changes meaning across the corpus: provider refusal behavior, an original user's request, an organization-wide invariant, or an operator-authored capability/data-flow manifest. Similar attack-success rates are therefore not automatically comparable.
- Most defenses are mechanism- and visibility-specific. A local refusal model, a provenance monitor, and an authorization reference monitor observe different evidence and enforce different authority sources.

## Coding rules

**Mechanism manipulated** records the intervention or system feature varied by the study. When a paper changes several features together, it is marked as a mechanism bundle.

**Authoritative policy source** records who or what ultimately defines permission. A provider's refusal behavior is not treated as equivalent to an executable application policy.

**Principal** means an entity whose authority or policy matters, not merely another LLM process. Agent/model counts are included separately where this distinction matters.

**Multi-agent causal control** is:

- **Yes** when the same task and policy are compared under single/full-context and multi-agent/distributed conditions closely enough to support an architecture-level causal claim.
- **Partial** when useful contrasts exist but multiple mechanism dimensions change together, or the baseline does not isolate agent multiplicity.
- **No** when the study has no such comparison, even if several models, calls, or roles are involved.

**Conclusion scope** distinguishes a mechanism-specific result from a scoped generalization. “Scoped” does not mean weak; it means that the conclusion should not be extended beyond the authority, observability, and intervention studied.

## Visibility, controls, and defense coding

| Paper | Defender visibility | Multi-agent causal control | Defense evaluated | Conclusion scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [DeCompBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13994) | Each victim call sees only its current subtask; conversation history is cleared, no cross-call monitor is present, and only environment state persists. | **No.** Monolithic prompts are compared with multiple stateless calls, so prompt granularity and context retention both change; this is not a single-versus-multi-agent architecture control. | None evaluated; the paper is a benchmark and leaves defenses to future work. | Mechanism-specific to independent-subtask decomposition under provider safeguards. |
| [Context-Fractured Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09084) | The local monitor sees the current prompt and immediate tool interaction but not artifact lineage. A trace-omniscient evaluator sees the full trace and artifacts as an upper bound. | **Partial.** The study varies memory/visibility and uses counterfactual probes, but decomposition, context reset, artifact transfer, and topology are not reduced to one matched factor. | Strong local judge and trace-omniscient upper bound are evaluated; provenance lineage tagging is proposed rather than established as the main evaluated defense. | Mechanism-specific to context fracture through artifact provenance gaps. |
| [Jailbreak-as-a-Service++ / PoisonSwarm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21184) | A provider ordinarily sees its own requests/accounts, not the coordinated campaign. Simulations vary local, regional, or global fingerprint sharing and provider guardrail strength. | **Partial.** Ablations and comparisons with individual models are informative, but decomposition, model heterogeneity, and provider isolation remain bundled. | Simulated fingerprint sharing, probabilistic provider guardrails, account banning/cross-provider coordination, and detection. | Scoped to distributed, cross-provider crowdsourcing; not evidence that every locally allowed/global harm trace shares this cause. |
| [Context-Fragmented Violations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22879) | Each agent sees its domain model. Sentinel sidecars mediate messages/tool calls, exchange signed provenance/constraint metadata, and issue boolean cross-domain policy queries without sharing raw graphs. A central checker is the stronger reference. | **Partial.** Cross-domain and same-domain cases plus centralized checks provide useful controls, but there is no clean randomized single-agent/full-policy versus fragmented multi-agent contrast for the same workflow. | Distributed Sentinel; baselines include prompt firewall, rule DLP, partial monolithic sentinel, LLM judge, and a central policy checker. | Scoped generalization to distributed organizational workflows with private policy fragments and mediated communication. |
| [MasDrift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07556) | Source anchoring sees the original request, public tool surface, and pending call. Chain enforcement sees carried parent policy and the delegated subtask. Offline scoring sees the complete trace. | **Yes.** The same 600 tasks and tools are run under a single agent and centralized/decentralized multi-agent topologies. Agent count, depth, handoffs, and tool exposure still co-vary within topology scaling. | Source-anchored reference monitor and monotone authorization-carrying chain, compared with no defense. | Scoped to authorization preservation and architecture-induced drift; the strongest architecture-level causal evidence in this set. |
| [ChainCaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26542) | A transparent MCP proxy sees mediated tool calls/results, source identities, sinks, and explicit data flow. It cannot see covert channels, hidden model state, OS effects, or tools that bypass the proxy. | **No.** With/without-defense runs use single tool-using agents; no matched single-versus-multi-agent comparison is made. | Monotonic sink-specific capability attenuation, lineage, explicit declassification, and fail-closed enforcement; compared with modeled alternatives such as scalar IFC/PFI. | Explicitly scoped to operator-modeled, proxy-visible data flow under trusted manifests. |
| [AgentFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22868) | A trusted reference monitor observes mediated tool invocations, delegation, modeled response sinks, provenance/taint, and session paths. Unmediated paths, covert channels, and unsafe text that never reaches a modeled sink are outside scope. | **No.** The evaluation compares framework conditions on agent benchmarks/replays; optional delegation is modeled but agent multiplicity is not isolated. | Declarative flow/path rules, task-scoped capabilities, runtime taint/provenance enforcement, and bounded SMT verification. | Scoped to configured, mediated, policy-visible flows; not a general detector for all unsafe behavior. |
| [Operational Reframing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07097) | The executor sees the routed task or planner output, sometimes framed as upstream-approved. There is no external trace monitor; a skeptical executor prompt forces independent safety review. | **Partial.** The five-condition design isolates useful routing contrasts, including direct and planner/executor paths, but the authors explicitly reject interpreting every adjacent contrast as a pure factor; it is prompt-only. | Skeptical-executor system prompt and intent-level evaluation recommendations. | Mechanism-specific; approval framing and pipeline aggregates do not warrant a general multi-agent composition claim. |
| [Breach by a Thousand Leaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02551) | Victim filters see individual queries/responses, not the adversary's accumulated knowledge or hidden global goal. The adversary observes and aggregates the transcript. | **No.** This is repeated querying of a victim model, not a single/full-context versus multi-agent comparison. | Information-censorship formulation and a randomized-response mechanism with safety/utility analysis. | Scoped to inferential leakage under a query-bounded adversary. |
| [Adversaries Can Misuse Combinations of Safe Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14595) | Each model/provider normally sees only the prompt routed to it; the adversary sees the cross-model transcript and global goal. | **Partial.** Combinations are compared with component models in isolation, but decomposition and heterogeneous capability/safety profiles change together. | No concrete runtime defense is evaluated; the paper recommends ecosystem-level combination testing and deployment risk analysis. | Scoped to malicious composition of complementary models, with ecosystem implications rather than a universal composition mechanism. |
| [Unsafer in Many Turns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13379) | The same agent retains the conversation and tool/environment state. Baseline safeguards must infer distributed intent; guardrails can inspect steps, while ToolShield supplies precomputed safety experiences. | **No.** Paired single-turn and transformed multi-turn conditions use one persistent tool agent, not a multi-agent architecture. | ToolShield's sandbox self-exploration and trajectory-derived static experiences; comparisons include external guardrails and taxonomy/no-simulation variants. | Scoped to multi-turn tool-agent transformations; cross-model/tool transfer does not make it a multi-agent result. |
| [Bounded Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888) | Trusted PDP/PEP components see signed delegation scopes/budgets, proposed and prior session actions, declared intent, approvals, and required evidence. Hidden semantics and cross-session composition are not fully visible. | **Partial.** Multi-hop attenuation invariants are tested at two to eight hops and with/without enforcement, but there is no matched information-structure test that isolates multi-agent delegation from a single-agent baseline. | Attenuating Policy Chains: per-hop meet, delegation budgets, action-history composition closure, intent binding, approval tokens, and fail-closed evidence checks. | Scoped to session-level delegation authorization with complete restrictions and serialized admission; incomplete mappings and cross-session coordination remain limitations. |

## Implications for the planned empirical paper

The closest precedent for the proposed controlled comparison is *MasDrift*, because it holds tasks and tools fixed while changing coordination architecture and includes explicit authorization semantics. The proposed work can still contribute by separating four mechanisms under a common policy, workflow, trace contract, and intervention framework rather than treating all distributed failures as one class.

Three design constraints follow from this audit:

1. Keep the authoritative policy executable and invariant across mechanisms. Provider refusal rates cannot substitute for local policy compliance.
2. Include a same-task single-agent/full-context control and an inactive-mechanism control for every mechanism. Most adjacent literature lacks one or changes multiple factors at once.
3. Match defenses to their actual visibility. Report whether each defense sees local prompts, full traces, artifact provenance, original user authority, cross-provider identity, or executable action history before comparing effectiveness.
